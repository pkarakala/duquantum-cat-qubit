Task 2.1: bit flip rate $\Gamma_X$¶

Answer. At the challenge cat size $\alpha=2.1$, $\Gamma_X=3.85\times10^{-7}\ \mu\mathrm{s}^{-1}$: one bit flip every 2.6 s. We get it two ways. Brute force time evolution works up to $\alpha=1.4$. The Liouvillian eigenvalue covers larger cats. Where both run, they agree to $10^{-8}$.

Definition. Start in $\lvert\alpha\rangle_a\lvert0\rangle_b$ with $\epsilon_d=g_2\alpha^2$. Evolve the full two-mode master equation, with jumps $\sqrt{\kappa_a}a$ and $\sqrt{\kappa_b}b$ and no elimination. Then fit

$$\mathrm{Re}\langle a\rangle(t)=A\,e^{-\Gamma_X t},\qquad t\ge2\ \mu\mathrm{s}.$$

For symmetric flips at rate $r$ in each direction, $\Gamma_X=2r$. Saved results load by default; set RERUN=True to recompute.

Scaling. A fit of $\Gamma_X=C\,e^{-\gamma\alpha^2}$ over $0.7\le\alpha\le1.4$ gives $\gamma=2.99$ ($R^2=0.9995$).

The simplest estimate is that one photon loss flips the cat through the overlap $\langle-\alpha\rvert a\lvert\alpha\rangle=\alpha e^{-2\alpha^2}$. That gives $\Gamma_X\approx2\kappa_a\alpha^2e^{-4\alpha^2}$. Its local slope $-d\ln\Gamma_X/d\alpha^2=4-1/\alpha^2$ is about 3 here, which explains $\gamma\approx3$.

At $\alpha=1.4$ the simulation is already 33% above this estimate. That is the first sign of a second route (Section 2).

Checks. At each $\alpha$, the fit is compared with the odd-block eigenvalue at the same truncation, an independent dynamiqs integration and other fit windows. The last column is the largest population on the top memory and buffer levels.

2. Beyond brute force: the eigenvalue¶

At $\alpha=2.1$ one flip takes 2.6 s, about $4\times10^{7}$ times the buffer time $1/\kappa_b\approx60$ ns. Resolving that in the time domain is impractical.

Every decay rate of a Lindblad model is an eigenvalue of its Liouvillian $\mathcal L$. The model is symmetric under $a\to-a$, so $\mathcal L$ splits into even and odd blocks. $\mathrm{Re}\langle a\rangle$ is odd, so $\Gamma_X=-\lambda_{\rm odd}$, the slowest odd eigenvalue. A sparse shift-invert solve (src/spectral.py) finds it at $\alpha=2.1$ in under a minute.

Two loss routes. (a) Squares are the time domain fits. Blue is $-\lambda_{\rm odd}$ at the largest converged truncation, up to $\alpha=2.5$.

  • Lose one photon (green): one loss through the well overlap, $\kappa_a\alpha^2(\coth2\alpha^2-1)\approx2\kappa_a\alpha^2e^{-4\alpha^2}$.
  • Lose two photons (pink): one loss pushes the state out of the code space, and a second returns it flipped, $(\kappa_a^2/\kappa_2)e^{-2\alpha^2}$ (Dubovitskii 2025).

Blue follows green for small cats and runs parallel to pink for large ones. Both lines are one-mode formulas, so they predict the slope, not the size. At $\alpha=2.1$ the one-mode model (buffer eliminated) gives $1.17\times10^{-7}\ \mu\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, close to their sum. The full model is 3.3 times faster because the buffer is slow (table below).

(b) The test of the handover: sweep $\kappa_a\times\{\tfrac14,\tfrac12,1,2,4\}$ and fit $\Gamma_X\propto\kappa_a^{s}$. One loss per flip gives $s=1$, two give $s=2$. $s$ reaches 2 at $\alpha=2.1$; the 2.05 at $\alpha=2.4$ is unexplained.

Result. $\Gamma_X(2.1)=3.85\times10^{-7}\ \mu\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, one flip every 2.6 s, converged to 1%.

The exponent is not constant. The local $\gamma=-d\ln\Gamma_X/d\alpha^2$ peaks near 3.1 at $\alpha\approx1.2$ and falls to 1.9 by $\alpha=2.1$ as the two-loss route takes over, consistent with the prompt's $\gamma\lesssim2$. The slow buffer makes flips 3.3 times faster than the one-mode model at $\alpha=2.1$, and the gap grows with $\alpha$.

Limits: no thermal photons, and the eigenvalue is not converged at $\alpha\ge2.6$.
