Task 2.3: transmon-free memory-bit readout

The task has three requirements. Each has a direct numerical answer:

Requirement Result
Show that the two memory states give distinguishable buffer fields Their final real fields are −0.01189484 and +0.01189484.
Use a drive well below $g_2$ We choose $\epsilon_Z=0.05g_2$, twenty times smaller.
Determine the settling time and whether the approach oscillates The response oscillates, then stays within the specified 1% tolerance from 0.968 µs through 4 µs.

Simulation and drive choice

Let $a$ and $b$ annihilate a photon in the memory and buffer. We use the supplied full two-mode Hamiltonian, adding the real, constant memory drive:

$$ H=g_2(a^2b^\dagger+a^{\dagger2}b)-\epsilon_d(b+b^\dagger) +\epsilon_Z(a+a^\dagger),\qquad \epsilon_d=g_2\alpha^2. $$

Both photon-loss channels, $\sqrt{\kappa_a}a$ and $\sqrt{\kappa_b}b$, are retained. Each initial memory state $\lvert\pm\alpha\rangle$, with $\alpha=2.1$, is evolved independently with the buffer in vacuum. Preparation errors are excluded. All supplied physical parameters are retained. Production uses $(N_a,N_b)=(32,12)$ retained Fock levels and double-precision Tsit5 integration with relative/absolute tolerances $10^{-10}/10^{-12}$. The 0–4 µs simulation saves results every 2 ns; this spacing is distinct from the solver's adaptive integration steps.

We choose $\epsilon_Z=0.05g_2=0.23970352$ µs⁻¹ to keep the displacement about each occupied memory state small. Its frequency scale is $\epsilon_Z/(2\pi)=38.15$ kHz, compared with $g_2/(2\pi)=763$ kHz. The drive sweep below checks that the response remains approximately proportional to this weak drive. This is a conservative working choice, not an optimized readout.

What signal distinguishes the memory states?

The drive slightly displaces the memory. The two-photon coupling converts the sign of the occupied memory state into the sign of the real buffer field. The leading estimate is

$$ \langle b\rangle_+\simeq-\frac{\epsilon_Z}{2\alpha g_2},\qquad \langle b\rangle_-\simeq+\frac{\epsilon_Z}{2\alpha g_2}. $$

The subscripts identify the initial memory state. Final values below are estimated by averaging 3–4 µs; they describe the observed interval, rather than an infinite-time stationary state.

Initial memory Simulated real field Predicted real field
$\lvert+\alpha\rangle$ −0.01189484 −0.01190476
$\lvert-\alpha\rangle$ +0.01189484 +0.01190476

The real-field separation is 0.02378968, 0.0834% below the predicted $\epsilon_Z/(\alpha g_2)=0.02380952$. A phase-referenced measurement of this real field can therefore distinguish the two memory states.

How long does the response take to settle?

The response oscillates before settling. Its first maximum occurs at 0.158 µs, 44.3% above the final signal magnitude. The oscillation period measured from successive maxima is approximately 0.313 µs.

We call the response settled when both complex buffer fields stay within 0.00011905 of their respective final values at every remaining saved time through 4 µs. This absolute tolerance is 1% of the predicted field magnitude $\epsilon_Z/(2\alpha g_2)=0.01190476$. Including both real and imaginary parts checks that the whole field has settled. The final values are the 3–4 µs means.

Both fields meet this criterion from 0.968 µs onward. The last threshold crossing lies in [0.966, 0.968] µs. Earlier crossings followed by further oscillation do not count. Tighter integration with 1-ns saved spacing and an 8-µs run using a later 7–8 µs mean give the same settling time.

Supporting physics: the imaginary field and the oscillation

The buffer-fluorescence readout mechanism and leading local estimate follow Réglade et al., Supplement S8.5. The following details explain the physics but are not additional Task 2.3 requirements. They are separate from the two plots needed to show the readout signal and its settling time.

Write $\beta_s(t)=\langle b\rangle_s(t)$ and $A_s(t)=\langle a\rangle_s(t)$, where $s=\pm1$ labels the initial memory state. Write $\bar\beta_s$ for the 3–4 µs mean of $\beta_s(t)$. The simulated final buffer fields are $-0.01189484+0.00311514i$ and $+0.01189484+0.00311514i$. The imaginary part describes the field component 90° out of phase with the real part. It has the same value for both states, so it does not distinguish the memory bit. Memory loss generates this background. With zero memory drive it remains $0.00304907i$, while the real-field separation vanishes.

The exact symmetry and local approximation below explain that shared background and why the response oscillates. Neither introduces a new readout observable.

For this real-parameter model, the antiunitary transformation $\rho\mapsto\Pi_a\Pi_b\rho^*\Pi_b\Pi_a$, where $\Pi_a$ and $\Pi_b$ are the mode-parity operators and the star denotes complex conjugation in the Fock basis, maps the two initial conditions into each other while leaving the evolution invariant. It therefore gives the exact relation

$$ \beta_-(t)=-\beta_+^*(t). $$

This accounts for opposite real fields and equal imaginary fields. The independent codeword evolutions test this symmetry numerically.

The exact first-moment equations are

$$ \dot A_s=-2ig_2\langle a^\dagger b\rangle_s-i\epsilon_Z -\frac{\kappa_a}{2}A_s,\qquad \dot\beta_s=-ig_2(\langle a^2\rangle_s-\alpha^2)-\frac{\kappa_b}{2}\beta_s. $$

Factorizing the moments and linearizing about $A_s=s\alpha$, $\beta_s=0$, define the complex amplitude deviations

$$ \delta a_s=A_s-s\alpha,\qquad \delta b_s=\beta_s,\qquad G_s=2sg_2\alpha. $$

Here $G_s$ is the local exchange coupling. The deviations are semiclassical amplitudes; the full simulation evolves the density matrix without moment factorization. To first order,

$$ \delta\dot a_s=-\frac{\kappa_a}{2}\delta a_s-iG_s\delta b_s-i\epsilon_Z -\frac{\kappa_a s\alpha}{2},\qquad \delta\dot b_s=-iG_s\delta a_s-\frac{\kappa_b}{2}\delta b_s. $$

Setting these derivatives to zero gives the local stationary buffer field

$$ \beta_{s,\mathrm{loc}}= \frac{-\epsilon_ZG_s+i\kappa_as\alpha G_s/2} {G_s^2+\kappa_a\kappa_b/4}. $$

To leading order in memory loss, its imaginary part is $\kappa_a/(4g_2)=0.00304718$, independent of the well sign. The zero-drive control confirms this background separately from the real readout contrast.

The local linear system has eigenvalues

$$ \lambda_\pm=-\frac{\kappa_a+\kappa_b}{4} \pm i\sqrt{(2g_2\alpha)^2-\left(\frac{\kappa_b-\kappa_a}{4}\right)^2}. $$

They predict an underdamped response with a 0.319-µs oscillation period and a 0.244-µs amplitude-envelope time. The local step response is tested against a matrix-exponential solution. Its approximate period agrees closely with the full quantum result, while the full quantum trajectory determines the reported settling time.

Validation and reproduction

The task script runs these checks before reporting success:

  • Positive and negative real buffer fields, exact conjugation symmetry, and zero real contrast in the zero-drive control.
  • Weak-drive response at $\epsilon_Z/g_2=0.025,0.05,0.10$.
  • Separate memory and buffer increases to $(40,12)$ and $(32,14)$, followed by their joint increase to $(40,14)$. A smaller $(24,8)$ case is also checked.
  • Tighter integration at relative/absolute tolerances $10^{-11}/10^{-13}$, with 1-ns saved spacing; an 8-µs horizon at the production tolerances.
  • Independent QuTiP BDF integration with separately constructed operators at the production cutoff $(32,12)$, isolating integration error from cutoff error.
  • Trace throughout the trajectory, final-state Hermiticity and minimum eigenvalue. Small signed numerical residuals are retained.

The [notebook](../notebooks/08_task_2_3_readout.ipynb) loads the saved results by default and can rerun the calculation. ## Measurement interpretation Let $\Delta\beta_R=\mathrm{Re}(\bar\beta_--\bar\beta_+)$ denote the separation between the two final real fields. The coherent signal flux carried by these opposite fields is $\kappa_b(\Delta\beta_R/2)^2=0.002311$ photons/µs. It excludes the shared imaginary component and incoherent emission, and sets the scale of the coherent readout signal. The reported time is the turn-on settling time of the conditional **mean** buffer response. A practical measurement must also integrate a noisy output record. Detector efficiency, amplifier noise, measurement records and single-shot classification are outside this master-equation calculation. Consequently, mean-field contrast does not establish a readout fidelity or an integration time. Likewise, the settled values describe local responses during a finite observation window. Eventual memory-bit switching can erase their initial-state dependence. The 8-µs check tests plateau stability over that interval; it does not establish an infinite-time stationary contrast or a protected bit-flip lifetime. Increasing the readout strength and quantifying that trade-off belong to Task 2.4.
